# Conflict Resolution -- "Tell me about a disagreement..."

> This is the **#1 most asked** behavioral question across all FAANG companies.

## Amazon LPs: Earn Trust, Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
## Google: Collaborative Nature, Intellectual Humility

---

## Top Questions (2024-2025)

1. Tell me about a time you had a conflict with a coworker. How did you resolve it?
2. Describe a time you disagreed with your manager. What did you do?
3. Tell me about a time you had to work with a difficult stakeholder.
4. Give an example of when you had to influence someone without direct authority.
5. Describe a time you had to build consensus across teams with competing priorities.
6. Tell me about a time your teammate didn't agree with your technical approach.
7. Tell me about a time you had to push back on a product decision.

---

## STAR Template

### Situation (2-3 sentences)
*"On [project], I was working with [role] on [what]. We had a disagreement about [specific technical/product/process decision]."*

Think: What was at stake? Why did it matter?

### Task
*"I needed to [resolve the disagreement / find common ground / make a decision] because [impact if unresolved — deadline, quality, customer impact]."*

### Action (this is 70% of your answer)
1. **Listened first** — "I set up a 1:1 to understand their perspective. They felt that..."
2. **Acknowledged their view** — "I agreed that their concern about X was valid..."
3. **Shared your perspective with data** — "I shared data showing that approach Y would..."
4. **Found common ground or escalated respectfully** — "We agreed to prototype both approaches and let the data decide..."
5. **Committed to the outcome** — "Once we decided on approach Z, I fully committed even though it wasn't my first choice..."

### Result
*"We shipped [outcome] on time. The approach we chose resulted in [metric]. More importantly, my relationship with [person] actually improved — we became go-to collaborators after that."*

### Learnings (for Google)
*"I learned that starting with listening instead of arguing saves time. Now I always seek to understand before seeking to be understood."*

---

## What Interviewers Want to See

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Listen before responding | Immediately argue your point |
| Use data to support your view | Make it personal ("he was wrong") |
| Show empathy for the other side | Badmouth the other person |
| Find a compromise or let data decide | "I just gave in" or "I forced my way" |
| Commit fully after decision is made | "I still think I was right" |
| Show the relationship improved | Leave the conflict unresolved in the story |

---

## Red Flags

- "I just avoided the person after that" — shows no resolution skills
- "My manager had to step in" — shows you can't resolve independently
- "They were clearly wrong" — shows no empathy or humility
- "I escalated to their manager" — shows poor peer relationships (unless truly necessary)
- No quantified result — story feels like a vent, not a professional example
