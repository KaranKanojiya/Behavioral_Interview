Leadership & Initiative — "Tell me about a time you led..."

At Senior/Staff level, EVERY story should demonstrate leadership — even conflict and failure stories. The key differentiator: did you create scope from nothing (identified the problem AND drove the solution), or were you just assigned a lead role? Interviewers reward the former much more.

Leadership at Different Levels

MID-LEVEL

Feature-scoped leadership

  • Leading a feature within your team
  • Mentoring a new hire or intern
  • Proposing a process improvement
  • Taking ownership of a bug or incident

SENIOR

Cross-team leadership

  • Leading a cross-team project
  • Defining technical direction for a component
  • Mentoring multiple junior engineers
  • Setting technical standards for your team

STAFF+

Org-wide leadership

  • Championing org-wide initiatives
  • Influencing VP/Director-level decisions
  • Creating technical vision others rally around
  • Force multiplier — making 10 engineers more effective
  • Create scope from nothing — identify a problem not on any roadmap

Initiative-Focused STAR Template

Situation

"On [team/project], I noticed that [problem nobody else was addressing]. This was impacting [metric/team/customers]."

Framing tip: emphasize that you noticed, not that someone assigned it.

Task

"Nobody had been assigned to fix this. I took it upon myself to [investigate/propose/lead] because [why it mattered]."

Action

  1. Identified the opportunity/problem — "I analyzed [data/logs/user feedback] and found..."
  2. Proposed a solution — "I wrote a one-pager proposing [approach] and shared it with [stakeholders]..."
  3. Built buy-in — "I presented to [who] and addressed their concerns about [risk/cost/timeline]..."
  4. Led the execution — "I organized the team, broke the work into [milestones], and personally handled [hardest part]..."
  5. Delivered and measured — "We shipped [what] which improved [metric] by [amount]..."

Result

"The initiative [reduced incidents by X% / saved Y hours per week / generated $Z revenue]. It was later adopted as a standard practice across [scope]."

Mentorship Template (Hire and Develop the Best)

Structure specifically for mentoring stories

  • S: "A junior engineer on my team was struggling with [specific skill/situation]..."
  • T: "I decided to invest time in helping them because [why]..."
  • A: "I set up weekly 1:1s focused on [topic]. I paired with them on [project]. I gave specific, actionable feedback on [what]..."
  • R: "Within [timeframe], they were able to [specific achievement]. They later [got promoted / led a project / became the go-to person for X]..."
Best signal: the person you mentored got promoted, changed teams voluntarily, or is now mentoring others themselves.

Example: "Create Scope From Nothing"

Example — Staff-level Initiative Story

Situation: I was on the platform team. Nothing was formally broken, but I noticed on-call load was climbing — the previous quarter had 47 pages, up from 22. Nobody had raised it as a project because each individual page was low severity.

Task: This wasn't on anyone's roadmap. I decided to investigate because if the trend continued, we'd need to add a second on-call rotation within 2 quarters.

Action:
1. I categorized all 47 pages from the last quarter into 6 buckets. 60% traced back to two flaky integration tests firing false-positive alerts.
2. I wrote a 2-page proposal: "Reduce on-call load by 50%". I outlined the two root causes, a 4-week fix plan, and estimated ROI: 25 hours/quarter of on-call time saved, ~$60K in engineer time annualized.
3. I walked the proposal past my manager, the two most-affected on-call engineers, and the SRE lead. Two engineers pushed back because they'd rather ship features. I acknowledged that and negotiated: I'd own the fix myself if they'd give me code review priority.
4. I fixed the two flaky tests over 3 weeks. In parallel I proposed and implemented a per-alert "did this need a human?" retrospective form.
5. I set up a weekly dashboard tracking pages per rotation and shared it in the team channel.

Result: Pages dropped from 47/quarter to 18/quarter (62% reduction). The retrospective form was later adopted by 4 other teams in the org. My manager cited this initiative as the main reason I was promoted to Staff the following cycle.

1

Red Flags

Claiming credit for the team's work: "I did everything" — false, and interviewers see through it. Show delegation and enablement.
No delegation: Did you just do it all yourself? At Senior+ that's a red flag — you should have been a force multiplier.
"My manager told me to lead this." — Show initiative, not assignment. Reframe: "My manager mentioned the problem in passing. I saw the opportunity and proposed to lead it."
Only leading easy projects: Show you led through difficulty — the harder the situation, the stronger the signal.
No measurable outcome for the person you mentored: "They learned a lot" is vague. Say "They got promoted 6 months later" or "They now lead the on-call rotation."

Follow-Up Questions to Prepare

"How did you delegate?"

"I broke the work into 3 workstreams and assigned each to the engineer best-suited: X owned the backend refactor, Y owned the migration script, I owned the shadow-traffic harness because it required the most cross-team coordination."

"How did you handle team pushback?"

"Two engineers wanted to ship features instead. I negotiated: I'd own the fix myself if they'd give me code review priority. That respected their time and moved the project forward."

"What if this had failed?"

"The proposal outlined a checkpoint at week 2 — if flaky tests weren't the root cause, we'd stop. I explicitly designed the plan to be de-committable so I wasn't spending team capital on a bet."

"How did you convince leadership?"

"I quantified the cost: 25 hours/quarter of engineer time in dollars, plus the risk of needing to hire a second SRE if the trend continued. Leadership makes decisions in dollars and headcount, not incidents."

Level-Calibrated Leadership Examples

Mid-level answer

"I built the user search feature, optimizing the query to reduce latency from 500ms to 80ms."

Senior answer

"I led the migration of our search infrastructure from Elasticsearch 5 to 7 across 3 services, coordinating with 2 other teams. This improved query performance by 60% and eliminated 4 hours/week of manual index management."

Staff+ answer

"I identified that our 12 teams were each building their own caching layer. I proposed and led adoption of a shared framework with pluggable backends. Cache-related incidents dropped 80% and freed ~3 engineer-months per quarter of duplicated work."
