Meta, Apple, Netflix, Microsoft — Company Values Compared

Each company evaluates behavioral interviews through a different lens. Tailor your stories. Meta wants impact + speed. Apple wants craftsmanship. Netflix wants judgment + candor. Microsoft wants growth mindset + empathy. Use the same 8-10 stories but emphasize different aspects.

Meta (Facebook)

move fast · focus on impact

Core Values

  1. Move Fast — velocity over perfection; ship and iterate
  2. Be Bold — take big risks; the biggest risk is not taking one
  3. Focus on Impact — prioritize what creates the most value
  4. Be Open — transparent communication, share information freely
  5. Build Social Value — make the world more open and connected

What Meta Looks For

  • Impact-driven stories — "I shipped X which moved metric Y by Z%"
  • Speed — how you shipped quickly without sacrificing quality
  • Data-driven decisions — using metrics to drive product direction
  • Cross-functional collaboration — with PM, design, data science
  • Willingness to take risks — and what you learned from failures
Key phrase: "The metric moved from X to Y." Meta wants to hear numbers, especially DAU/MAU/engagement deltas.
2

Apple

craftsmanship · simplicity · attention to detail

Core Values

  1. Excellence — the bar is perfection; "good enough" isn't
  2. Simplicity — make complex things simple and intuitive
  3. Innovation — think different; challenge conventional approaches
  4. Attention to Detail — every pixel, every interaction matters
  5. Secrecy/Focus — say no to 1000 things to focus on what matters

What Apple Looks For

  • Craftsmanship — obsession with quality and polish
  • Simplification stories — how you made something complex simple
  • Design thinking — considering the user experience holistically
  • Deep technical expertise — Apple values specialists, not generalists
  • Passion for the product — genuine enthusiasm
Key phrase: "I pushed back on shipping because the polish wasn't there yet, and here's what I did to close the gap."
3

Netflix

freedom · responsibility · judgment · candor

Core Values (Netflix Culture Memo)

  1. Freedom and Responsibility — high autonomy, high accountability
  2. Context, Not Control — leaders provide context; employees decide how
  3. Highly Aligned, Loosely Coupled — shared goals, independent execution
  4. Pay Top of Market — attract and retain the best
  5. The Keeper Test — "would I fight to keep this person?" If no, generous severance

What Netflix Looks For

  • Judgment — making wise decisions with imperfect information
  • Communication — articulate, concise, candid
  • Courage — saying what you think even when unpopular
  • Selflessness — team/company above personal goals
  • Impact — high-performing individuals, not just team players
Watch out: Netflix explicitly does NOT value "team player" for its own sake. They value high-performing individuals who also collaborate. Don't over-index on collaboration stories.
4

Microsoft

growth mindset · empathy · inclusive

Core Values

  1. Growth Mindset — learn from everything; everyone can improve
  2. Customer Obsession — empathize deeply with customers
  3. Diversity and Inclusion — diverse perspectives drive better outcomes
  4. One Microsoft — collaborate across teams, no silos
  5. Making a Difference — technology that empowers every person

What Microsoft Looks For

  • Growth mindset — how you learn from failure, adapt, improve
  • Empathy — understanding customer and teammate perspectives
  • Collaboration — breaking down silos, cross-team work
  • Inclusivity — how you ensure diverse voices are heard
  • Customer focus — solving real problems for real people
Key phrase: "I used to think X. But then [experience] taught me Y." Microsoft loves this framing — pure growth mindset.

Same Story, 4 Different Emphases

Here's a real example: you led a search infrastructure migration. Same story, 4 different framings depending on which company is interviewing you.

Meta framing — lead with impact

Emphasis

"I led the search infrastructure migration that reduced query latency by 47%, which in A/B tests moved search CTR up 3.2 percentage points. On our traffic, that's roughly $4M annualized revenue. I shipped it in 6 weeks — I shortcut the design phase by writing a lightweight proposal and running it past 2 senior engineers instead of full architecture review, which would have added 3 weeks."

Apple framing — lead with craftsmanship

Emphasis

"I led a search infrastructure migration. Two weeks in, I discovered that 4% of queries returned subtly wrong result orderings — the kind of thing that wouldn't show up in unit tests but that our users would feel. Everyone was ready to accept it as an edge case. I built a shadow-traffic harness that replayed production queries against both clusters so we could visually diff every result set. I refused to cut over until we fixed the last of the ranking regressions."

Netflix framing — lead with judgment

Emphasis

"On the search migration, one PM pushed to skip the compatibility layer to hit the deadline. I had shadow-traffic data showing a projected 12% CTR drop on the affected surface. I told him directly: 'I disagree with skipping it. Here's the data. If leadership overrides me, I'll commit fully — but the numbers are the numbers.' We kept the layer. He respected it, and we still hit the date."

Microsoft framing — lead with growth mindset

Emphasis

"I led the search migration. Going in, I underestimated the BM25 scoring differences — I thought they'd be negligible. When shadow traffic showed 4% of queries returning different orderings, I had to redesign the plan. I partnered with an SRE who'd done a similar Elasticsearch migration at their previous company. She taught me the compatibility-layer pattern I now use for every ranking change. I've since coached two junior engineers through similar migrations."

Do Your Homework Per Company

1-2 days before the interview: read the company's most recent all-hands memo, engineering blog, or CEO letter. Reference something specific in your closing "why this company?" answer. Recruiters and interviewers write this down.
  • Meta: Read the latest Zuckerberg annual memo, look at Reality Labs / AI investments
  • Apple: Watch the latest WWDC keynote — know what they're excited about
  • Netflix: Read the Culture Memo end-to-end (updated 2024). It's not marketing — it's a rubric.
  • Microsoft: Read Satya's book "Hit Refresh" excerpts on growth mindset
