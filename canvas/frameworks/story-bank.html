Story Ideas — Pull from Your Career

Think about times you:

  • Shipped something you're proud of
  • Fixed a critical production incident
  • Disagreed with your manager or peer
  • Failed and recovered
  • Mentored someone junior
  • Worked across teams
  • Made a difficult technical decision
  • Dealt with unclear requirements
  • Improved a process nobody asked you to fix
  • Advocated for the user against business pressure

4-Week Practice Schedule

WeekActivity
Week 1Write all 8-10 stories in the full template above. Fill in the Story Bank on tab 1.
Week 2Practice solo — record yourself, time each story to under 2 minutes. Use the STAR Builder to check pacing.
Week 3Practice with a peer. Get feedback on clarity, "I" usage, and quantification.
Week 4Practice adapting a single story to 3 different questions. Interviewers will re-ask variants of the same category.
OngoingRefine based on feedback. Update metrics if you remember better numbers.

Self-Check Before Your Loop

  • I have 8-10 stories in the bank
  • Every category has at least 2 stories (check coverage matrix)
  • Each story is practiced to under 2 minutes
  • At least 2 stories are failure/learning stories
  • At least 2 stories are cross-team collaboration stories
  • Every story has real quantified results
  • I know which stories I'd use for each Amazon LP
  • I've mapped 3 stories to Google's core traits
  • I've calibrated each story to my target level (see Level Calibration)
  • I have a "backup" story for the "So what?" follow-up
4

Reusing a Story Across Questions

The versatile story pattern: A single well-chosen story about a cross-team migration you led can answer at least 5 different questions:
  • "Tell me about your biggest project" → Impact & Results
  • "Tell me about a time you led a cross-functional team" → Teamwork
  • "Tell me about a time requirements were ambiguous" → Ambiguity
  • "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a PM" → Conflict
  • "Tell me about a time you delivered under pressure" → Impact & Results

This is why you only need 8-10 stories, not 50. Emphasize different parts of the same story based on what the interviewer asked.
