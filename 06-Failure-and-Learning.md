# Failure and Learning -- "Tell me about a time you failed..."

> Every FAANG company asks this. Your answer reveals **self-awareness, resilience, and growth mindset**. A candidate with no failure stories seems dishonest or lacking self-awareness.

## Amazon LPs: Earn Trust, Insist on Highest Standards, Deliver Results
## Google: Intellectual Humility, Receptiveness to Feedback

## Top Questions (2024-2025)

1. Tell me about a time you failed. What did you learn?
2. Describe your biggest professional mistake and how you handled it.
3. Tell me about a time you received critical feedback. How did you respond?
4. Give an example of a project that did not go as planned. What did you do?
5. Tell me about a time you missed a deadline. What happened?
6. Tell me about a time you committed a mistake. How did you handle it?
7. Tell me about a time you were 75% through a project and realized you had the wrong goal.

## STAR Template

### Situation
*"On [project], I was responsible for [deliverable]. The stakes were [why it mattered — customer impact, deadline, revenue]."*

### Task
*"My specific responsibility was [what]. The expectation was [timeline/quality bar]."*

### Action — Three Parts:
1. **The mistake/failure itself** (be honest, brief)
   - "I underestimated the complexity of migrating the legacy data..."
   - "I made the decision to skip integration tests to meet the deadline..."

2. **How you handled it** (this is the real answer)
   - "As soon as I realized the issue, I immediately [informed stakeholders/created a recovery plan/...]"
   - "I took ownership publicly in the team standup and proposed..."

3. **What you changed** (systemic fix, not just the immediate problem)
   - "I introduced a pre-launch checklist that the team now uses for every release..."
   - "I set up automated regression tests to catch this class of issues..."

### Result
*"The immediate impact was [honest — delayed by X days, cost $Y]. But the systemic fix I introduced has prevented [N similar issues] since then."*

### Learnings
*"This taught me that [specific lesson]. Now I always [specific behavior change]. In hindsight, I would have [what you'd do differently]."*

## Choosing the Right Failure Story

### Good Failures (pick these)
- **Real mistake with real consequences** — not a humble-brag
- **You owned it** — didn't blame others
- **You fixed it AND prevented recurrence** — systemic improvement
- **Reasonable scale** — not career-ending, but not trivial either

### Bad Failures (avoid these)
- **Humble-brag** — "I worked too hard and burned out" (not a real failure)
- **Someone else's fault** — "The PM gave bad requirements" (blame-shifting)
- **Too catastrophic** — lost the company millions with no recovery (scary)
- **Too trivial** — "I typo'd a variable name once" (not meaningful)
- **No learning** — "Yeah, that sucked" with no behavior change

## Example Framework

**Weak answer:** "We missed the deadline because the requirements kept changing."
- No ownership ("we", blame on requirements)
- No action taken
- No learning

**Strong answer:** "I failed to deliver the search migration on time. I underestimated the data compatibility issues between v2 and v3. When I realized we'd miss the deadline by two weeks, I immediately informed my manager and the product team with a revised timeline and risk assessment. I broke the remaining work into smaller milestones so we could ship the most critical features first. We delivered the core functionality on the original date and the full migration two weeks later. I learned to do a proof-of-concept migration with real data early in the project, not just test data. I now build this into every migration plan."

## The Meta-Lesson

Interviewers aren't judging you for failing — they're judging you for:
1. **Self-awareness** — do you recognize your failures?
2. **Ownership** — do you take responsibility?
3. **Resilience** — do you recover and keep going?
4. **Growth** — did you actually change your behavior?
