# The STAR Method -- Your Framework for Every Behavioral Answer

## What is STAR?

Every behavioral answer should follow this structure:

| Section | Time | What to say |
|---------|------|------------|
| **S** - Situation | 10-15% (~15 sec) | Set the scene: your role, team, project, business context |
| **T** - Task | 10-15% (~15 sec) | YOUR specific responsibility or challenge |
| **A** - Action | 60-70% (~90 sec) | What YOU did — steps, reasoning, tradeoffs, obstacles |
| **R** - Result | 10-15% (~15 sec) | Quantified outcome + what you learned |

**Total: ~2 minutes per answer.** Never exceed 3 minutes.

---

## The Action Section is Everything

Interviewers spend 80% of their evaluation on the **Action** section. This is where you show:
- **Decision-making** — why you chose approach X over Y
- **Influence** — how you convinced others
- **Technical depth** — specific steps, not hand-waving
- **Ownership** — what YOU did, not the team

### Use "I", Not "We"

| Bad | Good |
|-----|------|
| "We decided to refactor the service" | "I proposed refactoring the service because..." |
| "We shipped the feature on time" | "I identified the bottleneck in the pipeline and..." |
| "The team resolved the issue" | "I led the investigation by first checking..." |

---

## STAR-L (Google's Preferred Variant)

Google adds **Learnings** to STAR:

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **L** - Learnings | What would you do differently? What did this teach you? |

This shows **intellectual humility** — a core Googleyness trait.

Example: *"In hindsight, I would have involved the platform team earlier. I learned that cross-team alignment upfront saves weeks of integration pain later."*

---

## STAR-I (Impact Variant for Staff+)

For Staff/Principal level, add **Impact**:

| Section | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| **I** - Impact | Broader business or organizational impact beyond the immediate result |

Example: *"This reduced deployment failures by 60%, which the VP later adopted as a standard practice across all 12 teams in the org, saving an estimated 200 engineering hours per quarter."*

---

## How to Quantify Results

Always include numbers. If you don't have exact numbers, estimate and say so.

| Weak Result | Strong Result |
|-------------|--------------|
| "It improved performance" | "Reduced API latency from 800ms to 120ms (85% improvement)" |
| "The project was successful" | "Shipped 2 weeks early, adopted by 3 teams, reduced support tickets by 40%" |
| "Users liked it" | "NPS increased from 32 to 58; DAU grew 25% in the first month" |
| "It saved time" | "Automated a 4-hour manual process down to 15 minutes, saving ~200 hours/quarter" |
| "We fixed the bug" | "Root-caused a memory leak that caused 3 outages/month; zero incidents in the 6 months after the fix" |

---

## Common STAR Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's bad | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Too long Situation (3+ min) | Interviewer zones out | 2-3 sentences max |
| Using "we" throughout | Can't assess YOUR contribution | Replace with "I" |
| No quantified Result | Sounds like it didn't matter | Add metrics, even estimates |
| Choosing a "passenger" story | You were a bystander, not a driver | Pick stories where YOU drove the outcome |
| Only positive stories | Seems rehearsed, lacks self-awareness | Include failure + learning stories |
| Not answering the question | Story doesn't match what was asked | Pause, think, then answer the actual question |
| Badmouthing former colleagues | Instant red flag | Focus on YOUR actions, not others' failings |

---

## Practice Checklist

- [ ] Prepare 8-10 stories covering all major categories
- [ ] Each story practiced to under 2 minutes
- [ ] Every story has quantified results
- [ ] Every story uses "I" not "we"
- [ ] At least 2 stories about failure/learning
- [ ] Stories calibrated to your target level (see `12-Senior-vs-Staff-Calibration.md`)
- [ ] Practiced with a peer who gives honest feedback
- [ ] Recorded yourself and identified filler words ("um", "like", "basically")
