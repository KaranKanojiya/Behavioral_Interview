# Ambiguity and Decision-Making -- "Tell me about incomplete information..."

> This category separates Senior from Staff. Mid-level engineers follow direction. Staff engineers create direction from ambiguity.

## Amazon LPs: Bias for Action, Are Right A Lot, Think Big
## Google: Comfort with Ambiguity (core Googleyness trait)

---

## Top Questions (2024-2025)

1. Tell me about a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information.
2. Describe a situation where requirements were ambiguous. How did you move forward?
3. Tell me about a time you had to prioritize between multiple competing projects.
4. Give an example of when you had to pivot strategy midway through a project.
5. Describe a time you scoped a project from scratch with no clear direction.
6. Tell me about a time when analysis paralysis was slowing the team and how you unblocked it.
7. Tell me about a time you took an important decision without all the data.

## STAR Template

### Situation
*"We were starting [project/initiative] but [specific ambiguity — no spec, unclear requirements, conflicting stakeholder needs, new domain with no expertise]."*

### Task
*"I needed to [define the approach / make a decision / unblock the team] despite [what was unclear]."*

### Action
1. **Identified what you DID know vs didn't know**
   - "I listed the known constraints and the open questions..."
2. **Gathered just enough information (not all information)**
   - "I talked to [3 key people] to understand [specific things]..."
   - "I researched [comparable systems/past incidents/industry patterns]..."
3. **Made a reversible decision** (Bias for Action)
   - "I proposed we start with [approach] because it was low-risk and reversible..."
   - "I defined clear success criteria so we'd know within [timeframe] if this was working..."
4. **Set up checkpoints to validate**
   - "I scheduled a review after [2 weeks / first milestone] to course-correct if needed..."
5. **Communicated tradeoffs transparently**
   - "I documented the decision, the risks, and the alternatives we considered..."

### Result
*"We shipped [what] in [timeframe]. The approach I chose turned out to [work well / need adjustment]. The checkpoint process meant we caught [issue] early and pivoted without wasting [time/effort]."*

## Decision-Making Frameworks to Mention

### One-Way vs Two-Way Doors (Amazon)
- **One-way door**: Irreversible, high-stakes → take your time, gather data
- **Two-way door**: Reversible, can undo → decide quickly, iterate

### RAPID Framework
- **Recommend**: Who proposes?
- **Agree**: Who has veto?
- **Perform**: Who executes?
- **Input**: Who provides info?
- **Decide**: Who makes the final call?

### 70% Rule (Jeff Bezos)
- "If you wait for 90% of the information, you're too slow."
- "Most decisions should be made with about 70% of the information you wish you had."

## Prioritization Questions

When asked "how did you prioritize?":

1. **Impact vs Effort matrix** — high impact, low effort first
2. **Customer impact** — which option affects the most users?
3. **Reversibility** — do the irreversible thing first (can iterate on the rest)
4. **Dependencies** — what blocks other teams?
5. **Data** — what do the metrics say?

| Mid-Level | Staff+ |
|-----------|--------|
| "Requirements were unclear so I asked the PM to clarify" | "There was no PM or spec. I defined the problem, proposed three approaches, and drove the decision" |
