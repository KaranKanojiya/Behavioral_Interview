# Amazon Leadership Principles -- All 16 with Interview Questions

Every Amazon behavioral question maps to one or more Leadership Principles. The interviewer has a specific LP assigned to evaluate. Knowing which LP a question targets lets you frame your answer correctly.

---

## 1. Customer Obsession

> Leaders start with the customer and work backwards. They work vigorously to earn and keep customer trust. Although leaders pay attention to competitors, they obsess over customers.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer or end user.
- Tell me about a time you innovated and exceeded expectations.
- Describe a time you had to push back on a business decision to protect user experience.
- Tell me about a time you used customer data/feedback to drive a product decision.

**STAR Focus:** Show you prioritized the customer even when it was inconvenient or costly. Mention specific customer impact metrics.

---

## 2. Ownership

> Leaders are owners. They think long term and don't sacrifice long-term value for short-term results. They act on behalf of the entire company, beyond just their own team. They never say "that's not my job."

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you took ownership of something outside your area of responsibility.
- Tell me about a time you took a decision without the manager's prior approval.
- Have you ever gone out of your way to help a peer?
- Tell me about a time you pushed back on a decision from management for better long-term benefits.

**STAR Focus:** Show end-to-end ownership. You saw a problem, didn't wait to be told, and drove the solution.

---

## 3. Invent and Simplify

> Leaders expect and require innovation and invention from their teams and always find ways to simplify. They are externally aware, look for new ideas from everywhere, and are not limited by "not invented here."

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you invented something or came up with a novel solution.
- Tell me about a time you simplified a complex system or process.
- Describe a time you thought of an unpopular idea and convinced others.

**STAR Focus:** Show creativity AND simplification. The best answers show you made something complex simpler, not that you built something complex.

---

## 4. Are Right, A Lot

> Leaders have strong judgment and good instincts. They seek diverse perspectives and work to disconfirm their own beliefs.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you had multiple solutions and had to select the optimal one.
- Tell me about a time your team members disagreed with your approach but you went with it anyway and were right.
- Describe a time you changed your mind based on new data.

**STAR Focus:** Show your decision-making framework. What data did you consider? What tradeoffs? Why were you right? If the second question — also show humility about times you were wrong.

---

## 5. Learn and Be Curious

> Leaders are never done learning and always seek to improve themselves. They are curious about new possibilities and act to explore them.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you learned a new technology or skill to solve a problem.
- Tell me about a time you didn't know something and how you tackled it.
- Describe how you stay up to date with technology trends.

**STAR Focus:** Show genuine curiosity beyond your job requirements. Self-directed learning, not just "my manager told me to learn X."

---

## 6. Hire and Develop the Best

> Leaders raise the performance bar with every hire and promotion. They recognize exceptional talent and willingly move them throughout the organization. Leaders develop leaders and take seriously their role in coaching others.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you mentored someone and it helped their career.
- Give an example of when you saw a peer struggling and decided to step in and help.
- Describe how you've raised the hiring bar on your team.

**STAR Focus:** Show you invested in others' growth, not just your own. Specific outcomes for the person you helped.

---

## 7. Insist on the Highest Standards

> Leaders have relentlessly high standards — many people may think these standards are unreasonably high. Leaders are continually raising the bar and drive their teams to deliver high quality products, services, and processes.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you received negative feedback and how you responded.
- Tell me about a time you pushed back on a deliverable because it didn't meet your quality bar.
- Describe a time you committed a mistake and how you handled it.

**STAR Focus:** Show you raised the bar for yourself or your team, even when it was uncomfortable or created friction.

---

## 8. Think Big

> Thinking small is a self-fulfilling prophecy. Leaders create and communicate a bold direction that inspires results. They think differently and look around corners for ways to serve customers.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about your most ambitious project.
- Describe a time you proposed a bold idea that changed the direction of a project or team.
- Tell me about a time you identified a big opportunity that others missed.

**STAR Focus:** Show vision beyond incremental improvement. What would have happened if you'd played it safe?

---

## 9. Bias for Action

> Speed matters in business. Many decisions and actions are reversible and do not need extensive study. We value calculated risk taking.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you made a tough decision without consulting anybody.
- Tell me about a time you took an important decision without all the data.
- Describe a time when analysis paralysis was slowing the team and how you unblocked it.

**STAR Focus:** Show you acted quickly with imperfect information. Distinguish "reckless" from "bias for action" — mention the risks you considered.

---

## 10. Frugality

> Accomplish more with less. Constraints breed resourcefulness, self-sufficiency, and invention. There are no extra points for growing headcount, budget size, or fixed expense.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you accomplished a goal with limited resources.
- Describe a time you found a creative way to reduce costs without impacting quality.

**STAR Focus:** Show you treated resources (time, money, people) as precious. Delivered more with less.

---

## 11. Earn Trust

> Leaders listen attentively, speak candidly, and treat others respectfully. They are vocally self-critical, even when doing so is awkward or embarrassing.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you earned your teammate's trust.
- Describe a time you had to deliver bad news to a stakeholder.
- Tell me about a time you admitted you were wrong.

**STAR Focus:** Show vulnerability and honesty. The strongest "Earn Trust" answers involve admitting a mistake publicly.

---

## 12. Dive Deep

> Leaders operate at all levels, stay connected to the details, audit frequently, and are skeptical when metrics and anecdote differ. No task is beneath them.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you found a root cause that others missed.
- Describe a time metrics told a different story than what the team believed.
- Tell me about a time you had to get deep into the technical details to solve a problem.

**STAR Focus:** Show you went beyond surface-level analysis. You dug into the data, code, or process and found something everyone else missed.

---

## 13. Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

> Leaders are obligated to respectfully challenge decisions when they disagree, even when doing so is uncomfortable or exhausting. They have conviction and are tenacious. They do not compromise for the sake of social cohesion. Once a decision is determined, they commit wholly.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time your teammate or manager didn't agree with you. What did you do?
- Describe a time you committed to a decision you disagreed with.
- Tell me about a time you escalated an issue because you believed the team was going in the wrong direction.

**STAR Focus:** Two-part answer: (1) how you disagreed respectfully with data, (2) how you committed 100% once the decision was made, even if it wasn't your preference.

---

## 14. Deliver Results

> Leaders focus on the key inputs for their business and deliver them with the right quality and in a timely fashion. Despite setbacks, they rise to the occasion and never settle.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you couldn't meet your deadline. What happened?
- Tell me about a time you failed to meet your commitment.
- Describe a time you were 75% through a project and realized you had the wrong goal.
- Tell me about a time you delivered results under extreme pressure.

**STAR Focus:** Show resilience. Even if you missed a deadline, show what you did to minimize impact and deliver eventually.

---

## 15. Strive to be Earth's Best Employer

> Leaders work every day to create a safer, more productive, higher performing, more diverse, and more just work environment. They lead with empathy, have fun at work, and make it easy for others to have fun.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you improved the work environment for your team.
- Describe how you've promoted inclusion or diversity on your team.
- Tell me about a time you recognized your team's work wasn't sustainable and what you did.

**STAR Focus:** Show empathy and that you care about people, not just output.

---

## 16. Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility

> We started in a garage, but we're not there anymore. We are big, we impact the world, and we are far from perfect. We must be humble and thoughtful about even the secondary effects of our actions.

**Questions:**
- Tell me about a time you considered the broader impact of your technical decision.
- Describe a time you advocated for ethical considerations in a project.

**STAR Focus:** Show awareness of the bigger picture beyond your code or team.
