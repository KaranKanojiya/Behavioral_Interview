# Customer Obsession -- "Tell me about going above and beyond..."

> Amazon's **#1 Leadership Principle**. Google calls it "Putting Users First." Every company values this.

## Google: Putting Users First

## Top Questions (2024-2025)

1. Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer or end user.
2. Describe a time you had to push back on a feature request to protect user experience.
3. Tell me about a time you used data or user feedback to drive a product decision.
4. Tell me about a time you innovated and exceeded customer expectations.
5. Describe a time you had to make a tradeoff between business goals and customer experience.
6. Tell me about a time you discovered a customer need that nobody else had identified.

---

## STAR Template

### Situation
*"Our [product/service/feature] was [customer-facing context]. I noticed that [customer pain point / user feedback / data showing friction]."*

### Task
*"The expectation was [normal scope]. But I saw an opportunity to [exceed expectations / solve a deeper problem]."*

### Action
1. **Identified the customer need** (not just the ticket/request)
   - "I analyzed [support tickets / user sessions / feedback surveys] and found..."
   - "I talked directly to [N] users to understand their workflow..."
2. **Proposed a customer-centric solution**
   - "Instead of just fixing the reported bug, I proposed redesigning [feature] because..."
3. **Advocated for the customer** (even against internal pressure)
   - "PM wanted to ship without the fix, but I presented data showing [impact on users]..."
4. **Delivered the solution**
   - "I implemented [what] and measured [customer metric]..."

### Result
*"Customer satisfaction increased from [X to Y]. Support tickets for this issue dropped by [Z%]. [N] users specifically mentioned this improvement in feedback."*

## "Customer" for Backend Engineers

If you're a backend/infrastructure engineer, your customers are:
- **Internal developers** who use your APIs/services/tools
- **End users** affected by performance, reliability, availability
- **On-call engineers** who debug with your logs/metrics
- **Other teams** who depend on your system

Example: *"I noticed our internal API documentation was outdated and engineers were spending hours figuring out our endpoints. I created an auto-generated OpenAPI spec with examples, which reduced integration time from 2 days to 2 hours."*

---

## Red Flags

- "The customer asked for X, so I built X" — that's order-taking, not obsession
- No mention of customer impact in the result — just technical metrics
- "I didn't interact with customers directly" — find indirect customer impact stories
- Building what you think is cool vs what customers need
